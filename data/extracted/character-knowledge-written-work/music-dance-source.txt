Character -> Skills -> Knowledge: music and dance native source reconstruction

This file reconstructs the configured PE instruction bodies and their closed
selector vocabularies. It is source material, not a translation execution,
layout simulation, test result, build result or observed game display.
Descriptive aliases are not recovered native debug symbols. RVAs omit the
image base 0x140000000.

NATIVE FIELDS ARE NOT ALL FINITE STRINGS
Names of forms, compositions, instruments, civilizations, creators, deities,
creatures, rhythms and scale degrees are world data. The finite grammar
selects the field supplier; it does not enumerate world-generated names.
Generated form names may be empty when has_name is false. Instrument and
scale/beat words may contain CP437 language bytes; their name slots use the
existing native-language translation path. Finite English prose must not be
fed to that path as if an unrecognized whole sentence were a name.

COMMON NUMERIC AND LIST PRODUCTIONS
Cardinal 52e360 recursively handles zero..nineteen, hyphenated twenty..ninety,
hundred, thousand, million, billion and a negative prefix. It is used for
performer ranges, dancer/partner counts, step counts, named move/position
counts, passages, scale notes, rhythm lines and bars. It is not a vocabulary
limited to counts seen in a particular saved world. Existing inverse helper
adventure_location_count is shared by music/dance count parsing.
Ordinal 52eb70 has word forms Zeroth..Nineteenth; larger values and its numeric
mode use a decimal number followed by st/nd/rd/th with the 11/12/13 exception.
Native list punctuation includes comma with no following space, comma-space,
and, as well as; pronunciation parentheses can themselves contain a comma.
A separator inside a generated title or a pronunciation is not automatically
a boundary between semantic references.

MUSIC OVERVIEW: dd5ce0/dd0c50
1. Append generated name, then literal " is a ". Usage at form+118 selects:
   0 entertainment; 1 commemorating important events; 2 devotional music;
   3 marches and military engagements; default appends no usage literal.
   Usage 2 may append the worship prefix and the named figure at +11c.
   A missing/nameless figure after the prefix leaves the prefix in native text.
2. Provenance alternatives: non-minus-one entity/origin +80 gives
   " originating in " or " which grew out of the performances of " (kind 8);
   otherwise non-minus-one creator +84 gives " originally devised by ".
   An unavailable name does not undo its already appended fixed prefix.
3. form flag +120 selects improvised performances versus composer rules.
4. Walk every voice pointer at form+a0 in source order. Vocal flag priority is
   singer (bit 1), speaker (bit 2), chanter (bit 4); the fixed role is used even
   when the record also carries an instrument ID. Count fields +c/+10 emit A/a,
   an English cardinal, or cardinal " to " cardinal. Maximum >=2 pluralizes
   the role and selects recite instead of recites.
   Recited material priority is work +f8 -> "the words of " + actual title;
   otherwise poetic form +f4 -> optional "a composition of" plus generated
   form name; otherwise "nonsensical words and sounds". A lookup failure
   after the role has been emitted can leave a role without recite/material.
   Specifically, work lookup failure dd6118 -> dd6249 and poetic-form lookup
   failure dd6184 -> dd6249 bypass the verb and material but continue through
   the ordinary voice loop. Thus multiple bare roles, a bare role preceding
   a reciting role, and bare/reciting roles interleaved with instruments are
   native reachable output, rather than clipped text or invented variants.
   A successful but nameless form emits the recite verb with an empty material;
   an empty written title retains the complete "the words of" prefix.
   Adjacent vocal entries have only a space between them; there is no implied
   sentence terminator or conjunction. Each native count/role/recite tuple is
   a separate entry. Do not swallow a later tuple into the preceding title.
   Nonvocal records use their actual instrument definition, the same count or
   range, and singular (+68) or plural (+88) raw names. The first instrumental
   record introduces "The music is played on " or "while the music is played
   on "; later instrument entries use native list punctuation.
5. Aggregate voice component flags choose purely rhythmic; main melody only;
   melody with harmony; melody and counterpoint; melody/counterpoint/harmony;
   each applicable combination with rhythm. "The music" + "al voices..."
   are adjacent fragments producing "The musical voices...".
6. Entire-performance qualities, tempo, loudness and compound styles are
   independent finite selectors. The exact source appendix below retains
   every fixed choice, including relative passage tempo, mood and dynamics.
7. Melody and counterpoint phrase lengths are absent, short, mid-length,
   long or varied. They may share one clause or have distinct clauses.
8. Harmonic texture selects one pitch, intervals only, sparse chords,
   dense clusters or broadly layered chords. A repeat flag adds the
   "repeats for as long as necessary" sentence.
9. Scale/rhythm choices: no preferred scale or a named scale; independent
   free rhythm or named rhythm. Either may be omitted by its native sentinel.
10. Overall techniques iterate bit/enum entries: glide, grace notes, mordents,
    trills, rapid runs, local improvisation, melismatic/syllabic phrasing,
    syncopation, fills, tension/repose, modulation, arpeggios, legato,
    staccato and freely adjusted beats. Imperative and nominal methods are
    distinct native productions. Preserve the literal "locally improvisation".
11. Melodic-contour instructions combine independent frequency (sometimes,
    often, always), contour (rising, falling, rising-falling, falling-rising),
    altered degrees (flattened/sharpened plus ordinal), rise/fall direction,
    and optional technique list. Several instructions share one sentence.

MUSIC PER-VOICE, STRUCTURE AND PASSAGE BRANCHES
- Each named instrument voice or singer/speaker/chanter can always supply
  rhythm, harmony, counterpoint or main melody. Its performer style and
  articulation fields are independent and may coexist in one sentence.
- Voice ranges can stay/be confined to one register, cover the entire range,
  range between two registers, or cover the entire range between endpoints.
  Register names combine pitch bands with instrument-timbre vocabulary;
  dd4db0 scans its finite enum vector and supplies the selected descriptor
  token to the shared native word supplier 77cf20. It adds no prose frame.
- Structure introduction is simple, following structure or well-defined
  multi-passage structure. Each element has count/range and independently
  lengthy/brief/unrelated modifiers. Passage kinds are passage, introduction,
  exposition, recapitulation, synthesis, verse, chorus, finale, coda,
  bridge-passage, theme, variations. Theme/previous-passage references and
  first/second theme are separate slots. Repetition can be possible or fixed,
  repeat only the preceding element or the whole prefix, and be followed by
  additional structure elements. Repeated kind labels use productive ordinals.
- Each passage or each member of a passage group can be voiced by melody,
  counterpoint, harmony or rhythm of a named instrument/vocalist. A bare
  vocalist and a vocalist reciting a work/form/nonsense are independent cases;
  recitation can coexist with a melodic/rhythmic duty. Delimit a list only at
  another legal voicing prefix, retaining conjunctions inside a work title.
- Passage details repeat the overall style, tempo/dynamics, voice register,
  phrase length, harmonic texture, named scale/rhythm, performance method and
  melodic contour families. Per-passage techniques are often noun phrases.

MUSIC SCALE SYSTEM AND RHYTHM SYSTEM BRANCHES
- Fundamental scale construction: two chords from a divided perfect fourth;
  unevenly divided octave with quartertone spacing diagram; evenly spaced
  octave. Number of notes is a native cardinal. The diagram retains 1/O/x.
- Tonic fixed only at performance or passed teacher-to-student. Naming may
  name only one note, preferred notes, every note, chord roots or degrees.
  Single/plural name lists include optional spoken aliases and ordinal degree
  annotations in parentheses. Split lists outside parentheses.
- A named scale can be pentatonic, hexatonic, heptatonic or have no qualifier.
  Construction choices are selected fundamental degrees; two disjoint chords
  drawn from fundamental fourth division; two fourths; tritone plus fourth;
  fifth plus major third; joined fifth plus fourth. Chords have named lists.
- Trichord, tetrachord and pentachord descriptions enumerate degree choices,
  optional octave completion and fundamental-fourth/octave/semitone-octave/
  quartertone-octave degree systems.
- Global rhythm system can be polyrhythmic or polymetric. Composite named
  rhythms enumerate pattern references and an optional primary pattern;
  relationship clauses repeat polyrhythm/polymeter or distinguish common
  beat from common elapsed bar duration. A single rhythm line supplies beat
  count and optional subdivision into bars with a numeric pattern.
- Beat names include optional spoken aliases. Stress notation is an
  independently bounded paragraph: ! primary accent, X accent, x beat,
  - silence, ` early, ' late, | bar. Keep its notation line and legend,
  including when [B] makes the legend a separate paragraph.

DANCE OVERVIEW: 4ea820/4e83e0
1. Append generated form name + " is a " + usage/solo/group/partner
   qualifiers + " dance". Closed usages include sacred, celebration,
   participation, social, performance and war; source switch defaults append
   no qualifier. Participant clause is a single pair, count/range of dancers
   or count/range of pairs. Provenance has the same origin/performance-origin/
   creator alternatives as music. Flag +94 selects choreographer rules or
   improvised dancer guidance.
2. Accompaniment uses either a written musical-composition title (requires
   work genre 12 and a valid musical form) or a direct musical-form name,
   optionally prefixed by "a composition of ". These are distinct branches.
3. Two acted-out subject slots can follow in the same sentence:
   a) music-derived poem/work when the dance follows accompanying words;
   b) dance's direct historical-event caption or "the story of " figure.
   Each slot independently emits "as the dancer acts out" or plural variant.
   Slot b also exists with no accompaniment sentence. Empty lookups leave
   fixed prefixes; do not discard a second slot or treat it as part of the
   first title. Historical event text uses its native virtual caption supplier.
4. Overall and per-section formation: single line, several lines, circle,
   double circle, loosely mingled, or absent/default. Path is independently
   clockwise/counterclockwise (turning versus traveling for group/solo),
   improvised path or intricate path. Both slots can occur together.
5. With accompanying music, motion combines speed (very slowly, slowly,
   slower and slower, faster and faster, quickly, very quickly, absent)
   with the music generally or a named music/section rhythm.
6. Partners choose rare contact, open contact or close dancing. Intent cues
   are pushing together, pulling away, touch, light touch, visual or spoken
   cues; constantly/briefly can occur before or after the cue phrase.
7. A vector of partner changes emits every recognized entry, both for the
   entire dance (4eb4bb..4eb59f) and per section (4ed397..4ed489). Every entry
   repeats "with the lead" then one of: advance along/against the main line,
   turn out clockwise/counterclockwise. Separators are comma without space
   or " and ". It is not one selector per sentence. Unknown entries are
   skipped by native counting logic without replacing the known entries.
8. Creature imitation resolves a creature raw or the literal creature.
   Refined-art requirements count special positions and specific moves by
   actual vector size. A cardinal may contain several words, so the complete
   noun suffix bounds each numeric field.
9. Overall basic movement invokes 4e99f0; overall qualities and punctuating
   movement lists are independent style, action, transformation and actor
   slots. A [B] separator precedes each section with nonempty detail fields.
10. Music-tied sections begin with a named/numbered musical passage or enter
    a new section with it. Labels use introduction/exposition/recapitulation/
    synthesis/verse/chorus/finale/coda/bridge/theme/variations and independent
    first/second-theme or next modifiers. Otherwise a section gets a distinct
    identity and its own formation, speed, contact, partner change, basic
    movement, qualities and punctuating-action families.

DANCE MOVEMENT SUPPLIER: 4e99f0/4e75b0
Called at reference 4eb8ca, 4ed4ea, 4ee515 and 4ee570.
- Header flags select "called the NAME", fundamental move, fundamental
  position, one of the fundamental moves, or one of the fundamental positions.
  Singular native headers deliberately omit "is" before "the fundamental".
  A name field can be empty independently of the header flags.
- Movement-vector flags partition actions into sequential stages. One stage
  starts There; several stages use First, there / Then there / Finally, there.
  Position descriptions use should be; action descriptions use is.
- Action kind independently selects an article or a series of cardinal steps
  in a square/circular/triangle/figure-eight/intricate pattern. Style is a
  separate closed enum, with native a/an construction. Singular action words
  are move, turn, facial expression, hand gesture, straight/curved walk, run,
  leap, kick/left kick/right kick, leg lift/left/right, body level/level change,
  arm carriage, raised left/right/both arms, spin, independent body movement,
  sway, forward/backward/leftward/rightward bend, footwork, line-of-dance motion.
- Style includes every overall dance trait plus high, low, large, percussive,
  aborted and partially realized. Direction (clockwise/counterclockwise),
  turn extent (eighth/quarter/half) and repeated motion are independent.
- Transformations combine shadowed, performed in succession, performed in
  retrograde and mirrored. Actor slots refer to lead/follower/group members.
  Nested "with" action lists retain their own styles and transformations.
  Overall punctuating lists use the corresponding plural action vocabulary.

TRANSLATION BINDINGS FOR THIS SOURCE FAMILY
src/musical_form_descriptions.inc owns the musical sentence grammar;
src/dance_form_descriptions.inc owns the dance and movement grammar.
Both use normalized complete semantic paragraphs and the shared rich-document
rendering path; source separators are not independent screen-row translations.
Current fixes retain multi-entry vocal recitations and bare count/role entries,
including their interleaving with instrument entries; full partner-change lists;
both acted-out slots; no-accompaniment acted-out text; empty named/action slots;
and the recursive cardinal vocabulary. Count/role fields are parsed before any
opaque material name. Counted instrument suffixes likewise have priority over
an opaque recited-name fallback, so a trailing instrument is not swallowed into
a title. Each instrument-list member must contain a native count/range before
its raw instrument name; list punctuation alone cannot create a name field. They retain every recognized semantic
entry rather than turning later fixed clauses into procedural names.
All finite literal operands from the reference music/dance roots and movement
supplier follow, grouped by owning role. Their individual conditions and
selector destinations remain in the sibling TSV/ASM source files.
